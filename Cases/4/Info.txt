The case you are referring to is the Burari Case, which involved the mysterious deaths of 11 members of a single family in Delhi, India. 
Overview of the Burari case
Family: The Chundawat family, also known as the Bhatia family by their neighbors, had been living in the Burari neighborhood for about 20 years.
Victims: 11 family members, including the matriarch (aged 77), her children, their spouses, and grandchildren.
Incident: On the morning of July 1, 2018, their neighbors discovered ten family members hanging from an iron grill on the ceiling. The oldest member, the grandmother, was found strangled in another room.
Cause of death: Initial suspicion pointed to murder, but the police investigation eventually concluded it was a ritual mass suicide driven by "shared psychosis". 
Key investigation findings
11 Diaries: A major turning point in the investigation was the discovery of 11 diaries, which had been maintained for 11 years. These diaries, believed to be dictated by one family member, Lalit Chundawat, contained meticulous instructions for the rituals that led to their deaths.
Shared Psychosis: Lalit had become withdrawn after his father's death in 2007. He later claimed to be possessed by his father's spirit, which gave him instructions for the family to follow. Psychologists concluded that the family suffered from a rare mental illness called "shared psychotic disorder," in which they adopted Lalit's delusional beliefs.
Accidental Death: The diary entries indicated that the family did not intend to die but rather expected to be saved by their deceased patriarch's spirit after completing the ritual. The hanging was meant to be a religious ceremony for salvation, and the family members believed they would be untied by the spirit.
No External Involvement: Investigators ruled out murder or any external foul play. CCTV footage and forensic evidence confirmed that the deaths were a result of the family's own actions. 
Cultural impact
The mystery and chilling details of the case made it a national sensation and sparked widespread discussion about superstition, occult beliefs, and mental health in Indian society.
Media coverage: The case was the subject of the Netflix docu-series "House of Secrets: The Burari Deaths" (2021) and inspired the Disney+ Hotstar web-series "Aakhri Sach" (2023).
