Publicly reported evidence in the Burari case
The 11 diaries
The most crucial piece of evidence in the case was the 11 diaries found inside the house. 
Dictated by Lalit Chundawat: The diaries were written over 11 years, starting in 2007, and appeared to be notes dictated by Lalit, who believed he was channeling his deceased father's spirit.
Detailed instructions: The entries detailed specific rituals and instructions for the family to follow.
Matching ritual details: The diaries described the "badh tapasya" ritual, where family members were to hang like the roots of a banyan tree. The specific instructions—blindfolds, gagged mouths, and tied hands and feet—matched the physical state of the bodies. 
CCTV footage
Last moments: CCTV footage from a neighboring shop was recovered by the police. It showed family members bringing stools, wires, and other items into the house hours before their deaths.
No intruders: The footage confirmed that no outsider entered the house after the family returned, reinforcing the police theory of collective suicide. 
Post-mortem and forensic reports
No struggle: Autopsy reports revealed no signs of struggle or forced entry, suggesting the family members willingly participated in the ritual.
No foul play: Forensic reports ruled out poisoning and found no evidence of foul play. 
Interviews and psychological reports
Shared psychosis: Investigators consulted psychiatrists who diagnosed the family with "shared psychosis" or folie à famille, a rare mental disorder where a delusional belief is transmitted from one individual to others.
Interviews: Statements from neighbors and surviving relatives painted a picture of a normal family, making the incident even more shocking.
