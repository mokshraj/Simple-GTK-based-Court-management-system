Details about the Atul Subhash case
Atul Subhash, a 34-year-old AI engineer in Bengaluru, died by suicide on December 9, 2024, alleging relentless harassment, extortion, and false cases filed by his estranged wife and her family. His death ignited a national debate in India regarding marital laws, judicial bias, and men's rights. 
Background
Marital dispute: Subhash married Nikita Singhania in 2019, and they had a son the following year.
Harassment and extortion: According to Subhash, his wife and her family began demanding money from him in 2020. When he refused, Nikita left with their son in 2021 and allegedly filed nine police complaints against him.
Financial demands: Subhash claimed his wife initially demanded ₹1 crore for a divorce settlement, which was later increased to ₹3 crore. He also alleged she sought ₹30 lakh for visitation rights to his son.
False charges: The police complaints filed by his wife reportedly included serious allegations of murder, dowry harassment, and unnatural sex. In his suicide note, Subhash claimed these charges were false.
Judicial corruption: Subhash also accused a family court judge in Uttar Pradesh of accepting a bribe to rule against him. He wrote that the judge taunted him to commit suicide when he refused to pay. 
Suicide and aftermath
Suicide note and video: Before his death, Subhash wrote a 24-page suicide note, with "Justice is Due" written on every page, and recorded an 81-minute video detailing his harassment.
Police action: Based on a complaint by Subhash's brother, police arrested Nikita Singhania, her mother, and her brother for abetment to suicide. They were later granted bail.
Custody battle: The Supreme Court of India granted custody of Subhash's son to his estranged wife, noting that the grandmother was "virtually a stranger to the child".
National discussion: The case became a rallying cry for men's rights groups, who claimed it highlighted the misuse of dowry laws and judicial misconduct. Others argued it showed the complex realities of marital disputes.
Continued developments: As of November 2025, legal proceedings and public discussions surrounding the case continue.
