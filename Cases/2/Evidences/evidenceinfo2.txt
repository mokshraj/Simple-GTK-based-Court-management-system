Key evidence in the Atul Subhash case
Suicide note
A 24-page document: The central piece of evidence is the suicide note, which consisted of four handwritten pages and 20 typed pages.
"Justice is Due": The phrase "Justice is Due" was written on every page of the note, indicating Subhash's desire for a fair resolution to his legal and personal struggles.
Allegations: The note details extensive allegations of relentless harassment, false legal cases, and extortion by his estranged wife, Nikita Singhania, and her family.
Specific incidents: The document outlines numerous incidents, including his wife's demands for large sums of money for a divorce settlement and visitation rights to his son.
Judicial corruption: Subhash alleged that a family court judge and a court officer in Jaunpur, Uttar Pradesh, accepted bribes and acted with bias against him. 
Video recording
An 80-minute video: Prior to his death, Subhash recorded a long video detailing his ordeal, emotional distress, and the harassment he faced.
Shared publicly: He uploaded the note and video to a Google Drive folder and sent a link to an NGO and others, which was later widely circulated on social media. 
Digital files and data
Organized records: A checklist found on Subhash's wall indicated he meticulously planned his final act.
Checklist tasks: The list included tasks such as paying all his dues, scanning and uploading documents, and sending emails to various individuals and authorities, including the President and Prime Minister.
Evidence preservation: To ensure the evidence was accessible, he created data redundancy, saving his suicide note, legal records, and video in multiple locations, including his Google Drive and local storage. He also took steps to ensure law enforcement could access his devices, such as removing fingerprint and face recognition locks from his phone. 
Legal filings
Multiple cases: Subhash's note refers to the numerous cases filed against him by his wife, including allegations of murder, dowry harassment, sexual misconduct, and domestic violence.
Case documents: During the investigation, Bengaluru police collected documents from the family court in Jaunpur, where Subhash and his wife had been engaged in legal battles. 
Conflicting claims
Counter-allegations: After her arrest, Nikita Singhania claimed that Subhash's allegations were false and that she had been the victim of harassment and dowry demands by his family.
Lack of evidence: Subhash, in his note, pointed out that his wife had never provided evidence for the allegations of physical abuse or dowry demands
